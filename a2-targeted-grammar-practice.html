Part 1 — Past tenses

Lesson 1: Past Simple — finished time

Use the Past Simple for an action that happened at a finished time in the past.

PAST ───── X ───── NOW ───── FUTURE
X = a completed past action

Examples: “I visited Chiang Mai yesterday.” “She watched a film last night.” Look for yesterday, last week, two days ago, in 2024.

Remember: “I saw him yesterday,” not “I have seen him yesterday.”

Lesson 2: Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Use have/has + past participle for life experiences when the exact time is not important, or situations that started in the past and continue now.

PAST ─── experience / start ───── NOW
The experience matters now, or the situation continues

Examples: “I have visited Japan.” “She has never eaten sushi.” “They have lived here since 2020.”

Compare: “I have visited Japan” (experience) and “I visited Japan in 2023” (specific finished time). Ever and never often describe experiences; since can show when a continuing situation began.

Part 2 — Future forms

Read each lesson, then answer five questions.

Lesson 3: Going to — intentions and predictions

Use am/is/are going to + base verb for a plan you have already decided, or a prediction based on evidence you can see now.

NOW: intention or evidence ─────► FUTURE EVENT

Examples: “I am going to study tonight.” “Look at those dark clouds! It is going to rain.” Remember: I am, he/she/it is, you/we/they are.

Lesson 4: Present Continuous for future arrangements

Use am/is/are + verb-ing for an organised future arrangement, often with a time, place, booking or another person.

NOW: arrangement organised ─────► FUTURE APPOINTMENT

Examples: “I am meeting Ben at 4 p.m. tomorrow.” “We are visiting the dentist on Friday.” A specific appointment or booking often signals an arrangement. Sometimes both future forms are possible in real English; here, the context makes the intended answer clear.
